Why Nanotubes

See what one nanotube can do.

One sheet of carbon atoms, rolled into a tube. Five properties no other material combines.

Figures are single-tube laboratory values; composite performance depends on dispersion quality, loading and interface.

Read the history of advanced nanomaterials →

01 / Scale

Smaller than you can picture.

A single nanotube is a seamless wall of hexagons, one atom thick.

1.5 nm wide: about 50,000× thinner than a hair
02 / Strength

Pull it. It holds.

Every atom is bonded to three neighbours, so there is no seam to tear. At about one sixth the density of steel.

100 GPa tensile strength, ~50–100× structural steel
03 / Electrical

Current runs straight through.

In copper, electrons scatter every ~40 nanometres. In a nanotube they travel ballistically for microns at a time.

1000× the maximum current density of copper (~10⁹ A/cm²)
04 / Thermal

Heat races along the wall.

The stiff carbon lattice carries heat almost without loss.

3500 W/m·K thermal conductivity, about 9× copper
05 / Flexibility

Bend it. It springs back.

It recovers from extreme curvature. A steel rod of the same proportion kinks and stays kinked.

0 permanent deformation after release
In use

The same tube, eight uses.

  • Battery electrodes
  • Concrete additives
  • Thin films and EMI
  • E-textiles
  • Polymer composites
  • Sports gear
  • Water filtration
  • Lightning protection

One tube does all of this. We make billions of them work together.
