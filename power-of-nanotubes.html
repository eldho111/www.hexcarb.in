Why Nanotubes

See what one nanotube can do.

One sheet of carbon atoms, rolled into a tube. Five properties no other material combines.

Figures are single-tube laboratory values; composite performance depends on dispersion quality, loading and interface.

Read the history of advanced nanomaterials →

01 / Scale

Smaller than you can picture.

A single nanotube is a seamless wall of hexagons, one atom thick.

1.5 nm wide: about 50,000× thinner than a hair
02 / Strength

Pull it. It holds.

The same rising load on both. Steel necks, then snaps. The nanotube holds, at about one sixth the density.

100 GPa tensile strength, ~50–100× structural steel
Nanotube Steel bar
03 / Electrical

Current runs straight through.

In copper, electrons scatter every ~40 nanometres. In a nanotube they travel ballistically for microns at a time.

1000× the maximum current density of copper (~10⁹ A/cm²)
Nanotube Copper
04 / Thermal

Heat races along the wall.

Heat crosses the stiff carbon lattice almost at once. The polymer beside it barely notices the same touch.

3500 W/m·K thermal conductivity, about 9× copper
Nanotube Polymer
05 / Flexibility

Bend it. It springs back.

Both are bent hard. The nanotube springs back. The steel rod kinks and stays kinked.

0 permanent deformation after release
Nanotube Steel rod
In use

The same tube, eight uses.

01

Battery electrodes

Active particles store energy but barely conduct. A sparse nanotube web wires each one to the current collector.

02

Concrete

Concrete fails when microcracks grow. Nanotubes stitch across the crack faces and stop them early.

03

Thin films and EMI

Scatter enough tubes on a film and the network connects: resistance drops while the film stays see-through.

04

E-textiles

Nanotube yarns change resistance as they stretch, so the fabric itself becomes a strain sensor.

05

Polymer composites

A nanotube network pins the polymer chains at 1–3 wt%. The part stays stiff and sheds static.

06

Sports gear

Reinforced layups stay stiff at the same weight, and damp the sting of each stroke.

07

Water filtration

Water slips through the smooth bore almost without friction. Larger contaminants are turned away.

08

Lightning protection

Composite aircraft skins are insulating. A featherweight nanotube veil spreads the strike across the surface.

One tube does all of this. We make billions of them work together.
